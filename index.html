About the study

Why we are doing this study

Drop-off and pick-up are some of the busiest times on the roads near schools. Children and families are close to traffic. Our earlier study found that more drivers are distracted by their phones during these busy times.

This study tests two kinds of roadside signs that remind drivers to put their phones down. We want to find out which one works better. What we learn can help make more roads safer in the future.

The signs

The two types of signs

Both signs show the same message: PHONES DOWN. We are comparing them to see which one helps more.

Smart sign

Lights up when it sees a phone

A small sensor by the road checks passing cars. If a driver is holding a phone, a sign a little further down the road lights up with PHONES DOWN.

What it records: Only a count of how many drivers were holding a phone, and when.
Regular sign

Always shows the message

A yellow diamond road sign with the same message. Small lights around the edge flash to catch drivers' eyes. It runs on solar power and has no camera or sensor.

What it records: Nothing.

Both signs follow national road sign rules

Every road sign in the U.S. has to follow the MUTCD (Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices). It is the national rulebook for road signs. It sets things like size, color and where a sign can go, so drivers can spot it and read it quickly.

Our signs follow these rules. Town of Normal staff help place each one where drivers will see it in time.
