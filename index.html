Overview

Small signs, aimed at the riskiest minutes of the day

Every school morning and afternoon, traffic surges around Normal's schools. Our preliminary study found that distracted driving — handheld phone use and hard braking — climbs sharply during these windows, exactly when families and children are near the road.

This project tests whether simple roadside signs can change that behavior. Illinois State University is comparing two evidence-based approaches — a real-time smart sign and an always-on static sign — to learn which is the most effective, scalable, and practical way to keep monitored zones safer.

How the signs work

Two kinds of signs, one message

Both approaches deliver the same words — PHONES DOWN — but they get there differently. Comparing them shows which is worth scaling to more monitored zones. Every sign is designed to meet Federal MUTCD standards.

Smart sign · real-time

A sign that responds the moment it matters

A camera-and-infrared roadside detector spots a driver holding a phone, then triggers a downstream message sign that lights up PHONES DOWN only in that moment — exactly what the animation above shows.

Dual-component detector + blank-out message sign  ·  Sites S5, S6, S7
Static sign · always on

A clear message drivers always see

A reflective, fluorescent yellow-green aluminum sign flanks the school crosswalk with a constant safety message. It is the most cost-effective and fully compliant option.

Reflective aluminum  ·  min 24″ × 30″  ·  MUTCD R16-15  ·  Sites S3, S4, S8
7:20–7:50 AM  ·  2:20–2:50 PM

Signs are active only during these arrival and dismissal windows, for about three months. ISU student observers are sometimes present on foot to record driving behavior using a strict, safe protocol.

Placement: Signs sit roughly 150–300 ft ahead of each monitored-zone entrance or main crosswalk, so drivers have time to see the message and respond. Final locations are set with Town of Normal engineering staff under MUTCD sight-distance rules.

Privacy & ethics

What we collect — and what we never will

This study is built to protect the people it observes. It records patterns, not people.

  • ✓

    Only counts and percentages are recorded. No identifiable driver or student information is ever captured.

  • ✓

    Nothing is shared for enforcement. No individual data is shared with law enforcement or any outside organization.

  • ✓

    The only response is a sign. No citations and no penalties follow from observed behavior — the sole action is displaying Phones Down.

  • ✓

    ISU manages all the data. Data is collected, stored, and analyzed by Illinois State University alone.

  • ✓

    It follows established standards. The study meets ISU IRB requirements, IIHS ethical standards, and the NHTSA NOPUS observation protocol.

Roles

Who does what

What ISU will do

  • Procure, install, and manage the sign systems.
  • Train student observers in strict, safe, and unbiased protocols.
  • Analyze all data and prepare final reports with recommendations.
  • Share the results and findings with partners and the community.

What we ask of schools

  • Help keep school operations staff informed of the observation schedule and procedures.
  • No direct involvement is required from teachers, students, or families — beyond any routine communications a school chooses to share.
